Stop Cyberbullying

Everyone deserves to feel safe, respected and supported while using the internet.

What is Cyberbullying?

Cyberbullying is bullying that happens through digital devices and online platforms. It may involve hurtful messages, spreading rumours, sharing embarrassing content or repeatedly targeting someone online.

Online Harassment

Sending repeated unwanted or hurtful messages to someone through digital platforms.

Digital Respect

Treating people online with the same kindness and respect that we expect in real life.

Online Responsibility

Thinking before posting, commenting or sharing anything that could affect another person.

Common Forms of Cyberbullying

01. Hurtful Messages

Sending insulting, threatening or repeatedly upsetting messages to another person.

02. Rumour Spreading

Sharing false information or rumours about someone through social media or messaging platforms.

03. Online Exclusion

Deliberately excluding someone from online groups or activities to make them feel unwelcome.

04. Impersonation

Pretending to be another person online to embarrass them or damage their reputation.

05. Unwanted Sharing

Sharing someone's private messages, photos or personal information without permission.

06. Repeated Targeting

Continually targeting someone through comments, posts or online interactions.

Signs Someone May Be Experiencing Cyberbullying

Cyberbullying may affect a person's confidence, mood and willingness to participate in online or offline activities.

Emotional Signs

  • Feeling upset after using a device
  • Feeling anxious about online messages
  • Becoming unusually quiet or worried

Behavioural Signs

  • Avoiding social media or online groups
  • Not wanting to talk about online activities
  • Changes in daily routines or friendships

What Should You Do?

If you or someone you know is facing cyberbullying, taking safe and thoughtful steps can help.

01

Don't Retaliate

Avoid responding with insults or threats. Retaliation can make the situation worse.

02

Keep Evidence

If it is safe to do so, keep screenshots or records of relevant messages and posts.

03

Block the Account

Use platform blocking tools to limit unwanted contact.

04

Report the Content

Report harmful messages, accounts or posts using the platform's reporting features.

05

Tell Someone You Trust

Talk to a trusted parent, teacher, counsellor or responsible adult.

06

Seek Support

Remember that you deserve respect and support. You don't have to handle the situation alone.

Be a Responsible Bystander

A bystander is someone who witnesses bullying without being the direct target or person responsible. Bystanders can help make online spaces safer.

Don't Forward

Don't share, repost or spread hurtful content targeting another person.

Show Support

Be kind to the person being targeted and let them know they are not alone.

Report Bullying

Report harmful behaviour rather than encouraging or joining in.

Speak to an Adult

Seek help from a trusted adult if the situation is serious or continues.

Think About These Situations

Situation 1

Someone shares an insulting post about a classmate in a group chat.

Responsible Action:

Don't forward the post. Support the classmate and report the content.

Situation 2

A friend tells you that they are receiving upsetting online messages.

Responsible Action:

Listen without judging, encourage them to report and help them reach a trusted adult.

Situation 3

Someone creates an account pretending to be another student.

Responsible Action:

Avoid interacting with the fake account. Report it and inform the affected person.

Your Words Matter

Behind every screen is a real person. A small act of kindness can make a big difference. Use technology to encourage, educate and connect rather than hurt.
